# 2026-08-04 — fp8: the ceiling is 2×, and the roofline had the wrong row

## The measurement

Re-ran the 2026-07-29 roofline with the fp8 variants, same method — register-resident
`mma.sync` loop, eight independent accumulators, no memory traffic in the timed
region. RTX 4060 Laptop, 24 SMs, 96 blocks × 256 threads.

| instruction | measured |
|---|---|
| `mma.m16n8k16.f16.f16.f16.f16` — **what the encoder ships** | **35.6 TFLOPS** |
| `mma.m16n8k32.f32.e4m3.e4m3.f32` | **36.0 TFLOPS** |
| `mma.m16n8k32.f16.e4m3.e4m3.f16` | **72.0 TFLOPS** |

The first row reproduces `perf.md`'s 35.5 to within 0.3 %, so the harness agrees with
the one that produced that table.

⚠️ **Two corrections to `perf.md`'s roofline.**

1. It records `mma.sync.m16n8k32.f32.e4m3.e4m3.f32` at **41.6 TFLOPS**. This
   measurement gives **36.0** — 13 % lower, on a harness that reproduces the fp16 row
   exactly. 36.0 is also the physically sensible number: fp32 accumulation is half
   rate on GeForce Ada, and fp8 doubles the raw rate, so fp8-with-fp32-accumulate
   landing on top of fp16-with-fp16-accumulate is what the hardware should do. The
   41.6 wants re-deriving or retracting.
2. It never tested the variant that matters. **`f16.e4m3.e4m3.f16` is 72.0 TFLOPS —
   exactly 2× the f32-accumulate fp8 rate and 2.02× the ceiling the shipped kernel
   runs under.** The three ways out that §roofline lists ("fp16 accumulation, fp8
   e4m3, or a re-scoped gate") treated fp8 as worth 41.6/35.5 = 1.17×. Combined with
   the fp16 accumulation the kernel already ships, it is worth **2×**.

### The toolchain, and a correction

`mma` with `.e4m3` entered **PTX ISA 8.1 = CUDA 12.1**. `/usr/bin/nvcc` — the one on
`PATH` — is **12.0** and rejects the instruction with `Unexpected instruction types
specified for 'mma'`, which is what the first attempt at this benchmark hit.

⚠️ **This entry originally concluded that the project's build was therefore stuck on
12.0. That was wrong.** `nn/_build.py` deliberately ignores `PATH` and searches for a
toolkit whose major version matches `torch.version.cuda`; its own module docstring
describes exactly this trap. `toolchain_report()` says `nvcc 13.2 from
/usr/local/cuda-13.2`, and `build.ninja` for the shipped search extension confirms it.
**fp8 is already reachable from inside the project's build path with no change**, and
the proposed stage 0 is a no-op. Only a bare `nvcc` typed at a shell gets 12.0.

## What it would be worth

The encoder runs at 25.2 TFLOPS, **71 % of its 35.5 ceiling**, for 62.3k evals/s
boards-to-logits. Holding the same 71 % against a 72.0 ceiling is 51.1 TFLOPS, i.e.

    62.3k -> ~126k evals/s

which clears the project's 100k evals/s target on its own, having previously required
a machine rental. It is the largest single lever left in B2.

## The reason it is not free: e4m3 has a 3-bit mantissa

e4m3 carries **~2 decimal digits** — relative precision 2⁻⁴ = 6.25 % per element,
against fp16's 2⁻¹² = 0.024 %. A 256-long dot product with independent relative
errors ε per term lands near ε/√K on the sum, so ~0.4 % per GEMM, ~1.1 % over eight
layers, i.e. ~0.09 absolute on a logit of 8.2 — which the softmax turns into **~17 %
in probability space**. That is 80× the budget `validate.py` measured today
(max |Δp| = 2.2e-3) and 17× the 1 % target.

**Naive whole-network fp8 will not clear the accuracy bar.** That is a prediction,
and `brokefish/nn/validate.py` is now the instrument that settles it: it measures
exactly the quantity that matters, the prior over the node's legal edges, on a trained
checkpoint.

## What the literature does about it

**DeepSeek-V3** ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19437),
[Colfax analysis](https://research.colfax-intl.com/deepseek-r1-and-fp8-mixed-precision-training/)).
⚠️ The fp8 work is in **V3**, not R1 — R1 is the RL-reasoning paper and inherits V3's
training stack. Four transferable points:

* **Per-tensor scaling is not enough.** Tile-wise **1×128** for activations and
  block-wise **128×128** for weights. They report blockwise-only was insufficient and
  destabilised training. Our `d_model = 256` and `d_ff = 1024` divide by 128 cleanly.
* **E4M3 everywhere**, not the usual E4M3-forward / E5M2-backward split — fine-grained
  scaling buys back the dynamic range E5M2 would have provided. We are inference-only,
  so this is simply "use E4M3".
* **Keep the sensitive operators out of fp8**: embeddings, the output head, gating,
  **normalisation** and **attention**. For us that means the five embedding tables,
  `norm_f` and the eight LayerNorms, the softmax, and the policy/promo/value heads.
* **Accumulation is the trap.** Hopper's fp8 tensor cores accumulate at an effective
  **14 bits**, so they promote to fp32 in CUDA cores every 4 WGMMA (128 k-elements).
  ⚠️ We would be going the *other* way — deliberately accumulating in **fp16**, an
  11-bit mantissa — because that is where the 2× lives. Whether Ada's fp8 path has an
  additional internal limitation on top is **unmeasured and must be measured**, not
  assumed from the fp16 numbers.
* Reported cost: relative accuracy loss **under 0.25 %** versus BF16, attributed to
  fine-grained scaling plus high-precision accumulation.

**modded-nanogpt** ([repo](https://github.com/KellerJordan/modded-nanogpt)). The
speedrun uses fp8 for the **lm_head matmul only**, with an asymmetric rescale and
logit soft-capping — not for the transformer body. Two lessons: the single largest
matmul is where fp8 pays first, and — reported by leloykun on a record run — **the
fp8 scales affect wall-clock time as well as loss**, so the scaling arithmetic is not
free and belongs inside the kernel rather than around it.

## Proposed staging

Each stage is gated on a measurement, and `validate.py` is the gate for every
numerical one. Nothing here touches the tabula rasa boundary: quantisation is
numerics, and no chess knowledge crosses.

**0. Toolchain — already done.** `nn/_build.py` resolves nvcc 13.2 by matching
`torch.version.cuda`, not by reading `PATH`. Nothing to do; verify with
`toolchain_report()` rather than with `which nvcc`, which answers a different
question.

**1. Emulate before building (a day).** Quantise activations and weights to e4m3 in
torch — per-tile 1×128 and per-block 128×128 — run the existing fp32 reference, and
score it with `validate.py` on a trained checkpoint. This costs no kernel work and
answers the only question that matters: *what is the prior-space error at 3 mantissa
bits, and does per-tile scaling bring it under 1 %?* If it does not, stop, and the
answer is QAT before kernels rather than kernels first.
⚠️ Also emulate the **fp16 accumulator** here, because that is the half of the 2× the
literature does not endorse.

**2. One GEMM (days).** If stage 1 clears, convert the single largest matmul — the
FFN, `d → 4d → d`, which is ~⅔ of the FLOPs — and A/B it interleaved. Predict the
speedup from its FLOP share before measuring it. modded-nanogpt's evidence says this
is where fp8 pays first.

**3. The body (weeks).** QKV, attention output, both FFN matmuls. LayerNorm, softmax,
the embeddings and the three heads stay fp16 — DeepSeek's list, and they are 0.3 % of
the FLOPs anyway.

**4. QAT, only if stages 1–3 leave error over budget.** Fake-quantise the forward pass
in training so the weights learn to tolerate e4m3. This is a genuine change to `C2`
and would need its own A/B against a matched control, so it is last and it is
conditional — not a default.

## The honest expected value

The ceiling is 2.02×. Realised speedup will be less, because the kernel is at 71 % of
ceiling today and the scaling arithmetic is not free. The accuracy arithmetic above
says the naive version misses the bar by ~17×, and every mitigation in the literature
costs some of the 2× back. A defensible band is **1.4–1.8× at an acceptable error**,
with a real chance stage 1 says the error cannot be brought under budget without QAT.

Stage 1 costs a day and settles it. Nothing before stage 1 should be built.

# Stage 1 measured — 2026-08-04

`brokefish/nn/quant.py`. Emulated in torch, scored with `validate.py` on
`t7h-n128-collapse@2006` over 384 buffer positions plus the adversarial set. Deltas
are max |Δp| over each node's legal edges, against the fp32 oracle.

| variant | max \|Δp\| | top-1 moved | non-finite |
|---|---|---|---|
| **`off`** (whole tiled path, nothing quantised) | **1.53e-5** | 0.000 % | 0 |
| `W@448` | 1.54e-2 | 3.03 % | 0 |
| `W+A@448` | 2.39e-2 | 3.79 % | 0 |
| `W+A+acc16@448` | **inf** | — | **868,623** |
| `W@64` | 8.30e-3 | 2.02 % | 0 |
| `W+A@64` | 1.49e-2 | 2.27 % | 0 |
| `W+A+acc16@64` | 1.83e-2 | 3.51 % | **121,912** |
| `W@16` | 8.30e-3 | 2.02 % | 0 |
| `W+A@16` | 1.66e-2 | 3.02 % | 0 |
| **`W+A+acc16@16`** | **1.73e-2** | 3.27 % | **0** |
| `W+A+acc16@4` | 1.73e-2 | 2.27 % | 0 |

For reference, the shipped fp16 CUDA kernel measures **2.20e-3** on the same metric.

## Four readings

**1. The emulation is right.** `off` runs the entire tiled path — split into 128-wide
k-tiles, per-tile descale, fp32 accumulation across tiles — with no quantisation, and
lands at **1.53e-5**, one fp16 ULP. Every number below it is a cost of e4m3 and not a
bug in the tiling.

**2. The fp16 accumulator overflows, and the threshold is between 16 and 64.** At
`q_max = 448` every output is NaN; at 64 there are still 121,912. At 16 it is clean.
The module docstring predicted `sqrt(32) * (q_max/4)^2` would put 448 "at the fp16
edge"; measured, 448 is not at the edge, it is comprehensively over it, and 64 is too.
The worst case governs, not the typical one, because the max is taken over ~10⁵ rows.

**3. The risky half of the 2× is nearly free.** Once `q_max <= 16`, rounding the
accumulator to fp16 every 32 products costs **1.66e-2 → 1.73e-2**, about 4 %. This is
the part DeepSeek does not endorse — they accumulate in fp32 precisely to avoid it —
and on this network at this depth it is not what hurts.

**4. Activations are what hurt.** Weights alone are **8.30e-3**; adding activation
quantisation **doubles** it to 1.66e-2. That is the opposite of where the effort was
expected to go.

## The gate: not cleared

Full-body e4m3 lands at **~1.7 %** max |Δp| against a **1 %** target and the fp16
kernel's 0.22 %, and moves the best prior on **2–3.5 %** of positions — one node in
thirty would begin its first descent on a different move.

⚠️ My own prediction in this entry was **~17 %**, from ε/√K propagation. Measured
1.7 % — **10× pessimistic**. The propagation argument ignored that per-tile scaling
removes most of the dynamic range the naive estimate charges for, and that errors
across eight layers partly cancel rather than adding in quadrature.

W-only clears the target at 0.83 % — but W8A16 does not use the fp8 tensor cores at
all, so it buys no speed. The 2× requires W8A8.

## Three cheap things before QAT

QAT is stage 4 and costs a training run plus a matched control. These are hours:

* **Finer activation tiles.** 1×128 is DeepSeek's number for a 7168-wide model; ours
  is 256 wide, so a tile spans half the reduction. 1×64 and 1×32 are one constant.
* **Per-matmul ablation.** Four matmuls per layer; if the error concentrates in one —
  `linear2` reduces over 1024 and is the obvious suspect — keeping that one in fp16
  costs a quarter of the FLOPs and may buy the whole budget back.
* **Which layer.** Early layers have narrower activations than late ones in most
  transformers; a per-layer profile says whether excluding layer 0 or 7 is enough.

If none of them reaches 1 %, then QAT is the answer and stage 2 waits for it.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| `off` (self-check) | 1.53e-5 | 0 | 0.00 % | 0 |
| **`ffn` k128 @16** | **6.59e-3** | 8.5e-4 | 1.51 % | 0 |
| `ffn` k64 @16 | 8.55e-3 | 7.9e-4 | 1.51 % | 0 |
| `ffn` k32 @16 | 8.30e-3 | 7.9e-4 | 1.76 % | 0 |
| `all` k128 @16 | 1.73e-2 | 2.09e-3 | 3.27 % | 0 |
| `all` k64 @16 | 1.75e-2 | 2.08e-3 | 4.80 % | 0 |
| `all` k32 @16 | 2.09e-2 | 2.06e-3 | 2.76 % | 0 |

| `q_max` on `ffn` k128 | 8 | 16 | 32 | 48 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| max \|Δp\| | 6.59e-3 | 6.59e-3 | 6.59e-3 | 8.79e-3 |

## Three findings, two of which contradict what this entry proposed

**1. Finer activation tiles buy nothing.** The proposal argued 1×128 spans half the
reduction of a 256-wide model and should be too coarse. Measured: k128 → k64 moves the
stable statistic (p95) by 7 %, and k32 is no better than k64. The reason is structural
— every quantised activation here arrives straight out of a LayerNorm, so its dynamic
range *within a row* is already conditioned, which is exactly the job a finer tile
would have done. **`TILE_K = 128` stands**, and it is also the fastest option, since
the descale is amortised over four mma instead of one.

**2. `q_max` is flat from 8 to 32 and degrades at 48.** Below the flat region the
subnormal floor starts eating the bottom of the tile; above it the fp16 accumulator
approaches the overflow that was total at 64 and catastrophic at 448. **16 is the
middle of the flat region**, an octave of headroom on each side.

**3. FFN-only is 2.6× more accurate than the whole body**, not merely cheaper to
build: **6.59e-3 against 1.73e-2**, i.e. **0.66 %** — under the 1 % target rather than
merely under the 2 % the project will tolerate. Keeping QKV and the attention output
in fp16 costs a third of the available speedup and removes three quarters of the error.

## The settled configuration

    matmuls      linear1, linear2   (66.7 % of encoder-body FLOPs)
    format       e4m3 (float8_e4m3fn), weights and activations
    scaling      activations 1 x 128 online, weights 128 x 128 offline
    q_max        16
    accumulator  fp16 within a 128-tile, fp32 across tiles

    measured     max |dp| 6.59e-3, p95 8.5e-4, top-1 moved 1.51 %, 0 non-finite
    reference    the shipped fp16 kernel measures 2.20e-3 on the same metric

Stage 2 is a **go** on these numbers. Stage 3 (whole body, 1.83x at 1.7 %) stays open
and is within the 2 % the project will accept, but it should wait for stage 2's kernel
to price the error in Elo — which becomes possible the moment a fast kernel exists,
using the same head-to-head harness the terminal collapse used.

# Stage 2 machinery — 2026-08-04

`csrc/mma.cuh` (e4m3 primitives), `csrc/fp8_gemm.cuh` (the scheme on device),
`csrc/tests/tfp8.cu` (five checks, all green).

## What is pinned, and against what

| check | result |
|---|---|
| `cvt.rn.satfinite.e4m3x2.f16x2` byte order, vs `__nv_cvt_float_to_fp8` | OK, low half first |
| ... and that 1e4 **saturates to 448** rather than becoming NaN | OK |
| `mma.m16n8k32` 8-bit fragment layout, vs a host matrix product | OK, **worst delta 0** |
| `quantise_tile`, vs a host quantiser | OK, **byte-exact** |
| `gemm_fp8` on exactly-representable values | OK, **worst 0** |
| `gemm_fp8` on realistic values | 1.6e-3 relative |

The fragment layout was **not** transcribed from the manual and then trusted. A wrong
8-bit layout still issues, still produces finite numbers, and is wrong by an amount
that looks like quantisation error — so it is checked against a host product on
integers that e4m3 represents exactly, where the answer is an equality.

The same reasoning produced the two-case split in the GEMM test. With A and W in
{0, 1} every quantised value is 0 or 16, every product 0 or 256, and every partial sum
a multiple of 256 well inside fp16's exact integers, so the comparison is an
**equality whatever the hardware does internally** — and that is what pins the packed
weight layout, the padded activation pitch, and the two scale indices.

## ⚠️ A finding: the fp8 mma's internal accumulation is neither model

The realistic case leaves 1.6e-3 relative. The first reference summed each 128-tile in
double and left 1.3e-3. Modelling the accumulator as `fp16(C + exact_32_products)` --
what `quant.py::_tile_product` assumes, and what the PTX text reads like -- made it
**worse**, 1.88 against 1.52.

So Ada's `mma.m16n8k32.f16.e4m3.e4m3.f16` is doing something between the two. DeepSeek-V3
documents Hopper's fp8 tensor cores accumulating at an effective **14 bits** and
promotes to fp32 every 128 elements to compensate; this is consistent with Ada
carrying a similar limitation, and it is the thing this entry earlier flagged as
"unmeasured and must be measured".

It does not block stage 2 — 1.6e-3 on a GEMM is inside the 6.59e-3 prior-space budget
the emulation measured — but it does mean **`quant.py`'s emulation slightly
understates the kernel's error**, and the authority for the numerical question is
`validate.py` on the real kernel, not the emulation. That comparison is the next gate.

## Design notes that cost something to get right

* **`ldmatrix` is 16-bit only on sm_89** — no `.b8` form until sm_90 — so the fp8 A
  fragment cannot be loaded the way the fp16 path loads it. It does not need to be:
  the scheme requires a per-row amax and a convert before the GEMM anyway, so the
  quantised activations are written to shared memory *already in fragment order* and
  read with one 32-bit load per thread. The row pitch is padded by 4 bytes; without
  it, the eight lanes sharing a `t` stride by the row pitch and every load is a 4-way
  bank conflict.
* **D and A no longer agree.** The fp16 kernel chains a GEMM result into the next mma
  because D's registers *are* an A fragment. At 8 bits an A fragment is four registers
  of sixteen values and a D fragment is two of four, so the chain must pass through
  quantisation — which the scheme was paying for regardless.
* **`a_pitch` had to be `__host__ __device__`.** The packer and whoever sizes the
  shared-memory allocation both need it, and a pitch computed in two places is a pitch
  that will eventually disagree.

`tests/` is **358 passed, 19 skipped**; `tests/test_model.py` and `tests/test_b2.py`
are green after `mma.cuh` changed, so the shipped fp16 encoder is unaffected.

Not yet done: packing the FFN weights from Python, wiring `gemm_fp8` into

# Stage 2 measured: 1.70x on the GEMM — 2026-08-04

Interleaved A/B of `gemm_direct` against `gemm_fp8` on `ff1`'s real shape — 32 tokens
x [1024, 256], 8 warps, four passes of four n-tiles, the same launch shape encoder.cu
uses — 48 CTAs, 2000 reps, six rounds alternating order.

| | time | achieved | of its ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|
| `gemm_direct` (fp16) | 51.38 ms | **31.3 TFLOPS** | 88 % of 35.6 |
| `gemm_fp8` (e4m3) | 30.15 ms | **53.4 TFLOPS** | 74 % of 72.0 |
| | | | **1.70x** |

Below the 1.83x arithmetic ratio, and the reason is visible in the last column: the
fp16 tile is already at 88 % of what its instruction can issue, and the fp8 tile is at
74 % of what its instruction can. The scaling and descale work is real.

**Projected onto the whole encoder**: the FFN is 66.7 % of body FLOPs and the body is
98 % of the network, so `1 / (0.347 + 0.653 / 1.70)` = **1.37x**, or 62.3k -> ~85k
evals/s. ⚠️ That is a projection with one known bias: this benchmark has no
LayerNorm, no attention, no barriers and no residual traffic, and the fp16 tile hits
88 % of ceiling here against the megakernel's measured 71 %. The encoder-level number
is not knowable without the integration.

## Registers, and the SMEM constraint that shaped the design

`-Xptxas -v`: **78 registers, 0 spills** for the fp8 GEMM kernel including the fp32
accumulator. Two blocks per SM allows 128 at 256 threads, so the accumulator does not
cost occupancy — the concern that motivated measuring it was unfounded.

Shared memory was the real constraint and it very nearly bit. encoder.cu's budget is a
`static_assert` at **50,176 B**, "the largest block that still fits twice on an sm89
SM", and it is exactly full. A separate byte buffer for quantised activations needed
8,320 B and would have cost the second CTA — worth 5.4 % per perf.md, out of a 37 %
gain.

The way out costs nothing: **quantise in place**, writing the e4m3 bytes over the
front of the fp16 row that produced them. A row's 256 bytes land inside the 512 bytes
just read into registers, rows are independent, and one `__syncwarp` orders it. Reusing
the fp16 row pitch of 528 B is also *better* for banks than a packed 260: 528 / 4 = 132
words, 132 mod 32 = 4, so the eight lanes sharing a `t` hit eight distinct banks. The
per-row scales fit in the 16 bytes of row padding that already exist. The benchmark
above allocates 17,152 B against the fp16 path's 16,896 — the difference is only the
scale array, and it fits in the padding in the real kernel.

## ⚠️ Three benchmark bugs in a row, all of which read as success

1. **Buffer aliasing** (yesterday): `cuda.forward_full` returns views it overwrites, so
   a chunked caller measured the last chunk four times. Read as a 950x kernel error.
2. **Loop hoisting**: with loop-invariant inputs ptxas computed the GEMM once. Reported
   **171 TFLOPS** for fp16 against a 35.6 ceiling.
3. **Dead-code elimination**: reading `acc[0][0][0]` let ptxas delete the other seven
   accumulators. Still reported **156 TFLOPS**.

Each produced a plausible-looking speedup — 1.15x, then 1.22x — and only the
*impossible TFLOPS* gave them away. The lesson is cheap and general: **a throughput
benchmark must be checked against the machine's ceiling before its ratio is believed**,
because the ratio survives all three of these and the absolute number does not.

## What remains

The machinery is built and tested; what is left is wiring it into encoder.cu:

* a second weight blob and `Off`-style offsets for the packed fp8 FFN weights;
* `quantise_tile` over `bufB` before the chunk loop and over `hid` inside it, in place;
* the two `gemm_direct` calls swapped for `gemm_fp8`, behind a build flag so the fp16
  path stays the default;
* packing and uploading from `nn/cuda_impl.py`;
* then `validate.py` on the real kernel — which is the gate, because the emulation was
  shown above to *understate* the kernel's error — and `bench_model` for the encoder
  number.

Stopping here rather than pushing a half-verified change into the megakernel: it is the
one file where a silent error costs a training run rather than a test.
