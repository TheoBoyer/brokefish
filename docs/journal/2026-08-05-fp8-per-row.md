# 2026-08-05 — one scale per row: the fp32 accumulator was the whole cost

## Where the day started

The fp8 FFN shipped at **1.154×** and the isolated `gemm_fp8` measured **1.67×** on
the ff1 shape. That gap was the question. `-Xptxas -v` named the suspect:

```
encoder_kernel<false>   128 registers,  12 B spill stores,   64 B spill loads
encoder_kernel<true>    128 registers, 160 B spill stores,  960 B spill loads
```

The fp8 path carried `float acc[2][4][4]` — a running total across k-tiles, on top of
the fp16 mma fragment — because the activation scale changed every 128 k-elements and
each tile had to be descaled before it could be added to the next. 32 registers where
the fp16 path needs 16.

## The obvious fix is a measured loss

`__launch_bounds__(THREADS, FP8 ? 1 : 2)` lifts the cap to 255 for the fp8
instantiation alone. It works exactly as advertised — **200 registers, 0 B of spill** —
and the kernel gets **slower**:

| | registers | spill loads | vs fp16 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 CTAs/SM (ships) | 128 | 960 B | **1.154×** |
| 1 CTA/SM | 200 | 0 B | **0.978×** |

Two CTAs per SM is 16 warps and that is the entire latency-hiding budget on this
kernel; halving it costs far more than local-memory traffic ever did. And 128 is not a
tuning knob: 65536 registers / (2 CTAs × 256 threads) = 128 exactly. **The only way out
is to need fewer registers, not to be given more.**

## What made that affordable

A single scale for the whole 256-deep reduction lets every k-group share it, so the
fp16 mma fragment *is* the accumulator and one descale at the end suffices — no fp32
array, no per-tile promotion. The question was what the coarser scale costs.

Emulated first, through `nn/quant.py` on `t7h-fp8-002206`, 2021 non-terminal positions:

| FFN scheme | max abs Δp | p95 | top-1 moved |
|---|---|---|---|
| per 128-element k-tile (shipped) | 1.51e-2 | 1.28e-3 | 1.336 % |
| **per row** | 1.83e-2 | **1.25e-3** | **1.138 %** |

The headline gets slightly worse and both statistics the search actually consumes get
better. A max over ~194k edges is a tail; p95 and top-1 are the distribution.

That result is consistent with the older finding that finer tiles bought nothing: the
activations come straight out of a LayerNorm, so a row's k-tiles are alike and a
per-tile scale is tracking noise.

## The accumulator bound is what sets q_max, and it moved

One fp16 accumulator now spans `kRowK = 256` products instead of 128, so the worst case
doubles: `q_max² · 256 ≤ 65504` gives `q_max ≤ 15.99` — no margin at 16. **`kQMax` went
16 → 8**, which is 16384 and a 4× margin, and the prior-space sweep found 4, 6 and 8
indistinguishable, so it is free.

⚠️ This constant now has a third consumer. `_pack_fp8` must pass **`tile_k = K`** as
well as `Q_MAX`: `gemm_fp8_row` reads `w_scale[n8 / 16]` with no k index at all, so a
packer still emitting a scale per 128 k-elements would have every output scaled by
k-tile 0's constant — in range, finite, and wrong by something that looks like
quantisation error. `tests/test_quant.py` pins both the value and the shape.

## Measured

`bench` is `speed.py`, order-balanced over six rounds, B = 4096, on
`t7h-n128-collapse-002006`. Validation is `nn/validate.py` in prior space on
`t7h-fp8-002206`, 2041 non-terminal positions including the adversarial set.

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| spill loads | 960 B | **200 B** |
| registers / CTAs per SM | 128 / 2 | 128 / 2 |
| throughput | 1.154× | **1.214×** = 82.2k evals/s |
| max abs Δp | 1.05e-2 | 1.81e-2 |
| p95 Δp | — | 1.22e-3 |
| top-1 moved | — | 1.32 % |

Repeated on a hotter card: ×1.218 (fp16 65.8k, fp8 80.2k). The ratio is stable to
0.3 % while the absolute moves 2.8 %, which is why the ledger records ratios.

**Predicted 79–84k from the measured time budget; landed at 82.2k.** The budget was
`other 20.6 % / qkv 19.9 % / out 6.6 % / FFN 52.9 %`, measured by timing all four real
GEMM shapes (`csrc/shapes_ab.cu`, 30.6–30.7 TFLOPS each, 32.1 for a whole layer against
the kernel's 25.5).

And the kernel now lands on its own specification: quant.py predicted 1.83e-2 / 1.25e-3
/ 1.14 % and the kernel measures 1.81e-2 / 1.22e-3 / 1.32 %. The per-128-tile version
was **1.6× worse than its own prediction**; losing seven of the eight descales is what
closed that, which is circumstantial evidence that the divergence lived in the
promote-and-descale step rather than in the mma.

## What did not work, and why it is worth writing down

**FlashAttention-3's incoherent processing does not transfer.** Its premise holds here
— our tiles really are spiky, amax/rms 5.1 at qkv and 6.7 at ffn2 against a Gaussian
2.9, and a random-sign Hadamard flattens every one of them to ~2.7. It buys nothing:

| | max abs Δp | p95 | top-1 moved |
|---|---|---|---|
| fp8 FFN | 1.51e-2 | 1.28e-3 | 1.336 % |
| fp8 FFN + hadamard | **2.69e-2** | 1.28e-3 | 0.990 % |
| fp8 whole body | 4.10e-2 | 2.87e-3 | 2.969 % |
| fp8 whole body + hadamard | **4.30e-2** | 2.96e-3 | 2.573 % |

e4m3's precision is *relative* — 2⁻⁴ per element, scale-free across eighteen binades.
Incoherent processing pays when a few huge entries crush the rest into subnormals;
FA3's benchmark injects 0.1 % outliers to create exactly that. At amax/rms = 7 our
elements span about four binades inside an eighteen-binade normal range, so there is no
underflow to recover and flattening the tile changes nothing but the scale.

**Extending fp8 past the FFN is closed on error, not on speed.** Per-matmul
attribution, same positions:

| scope | FLOP share | max abs Δp | top-1 moved |
|---|---|---|---|
| ffn1 | 8/24 | 1.42e-2 | 0.792 % |
| ffn2 | 8/24 | 8.30e-3 | 0.643 % |
| out | 2/24 | 2.05e-2 | 1.188 % |
| **qkv** | 6/24 | **4.64e-2** | 2.474 % |

`qkv` alone costs three times the FFN's error per FLOP, because its output feeds a
softmax that exponentiates it — DeepSeek-V3's exclusion list, arrived at independently.
`out` is 8 % of the FLOPs for 2.05e-2, a worse trade still.

## What this leaves

FFN-only fp8 tops out near **84k**: at fp8's hard 2.0× the FFN term saturates and the
whole-kernel bound is 1.40×. 90k needs the non-matmul 20.6 % cut to 13.0 %, which is
the CODA reparametrisation (arXiv 2605.19269) — γ folded into the next GEMM's weights,
β into its bias, μ deferred as a rank-1 correction, and 1/σ deferred to the epilogue so
the LayerNorm reduction leaves the critical path and `bufB` is never materialised.

⚠️ **Before any of that, the 20.6 % needs decomposing.** Its size is measured; its
composition is not, and promising a 37 % cut out of a bucket whose contents are unknown
is arithmetic, not a plan.

## Loose end

`nn/validate.py` reports the fp16 `cuda` path keeping a different §4.3 edge set on **1
of 2041** positions. Pre-existing and not from this change — the fp16 instantiation's
register report is byte-identical before and after — and it is the known 218-move
adversarial position where the truncation ties differently in fp16 than in fp32.
