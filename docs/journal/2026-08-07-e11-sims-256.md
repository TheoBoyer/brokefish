# 2026-08-07 (eighth entry) — E1.1 answered from checkpoints that already existed

`sims = 256` was **already run** — `t24h-n256`, 2026-08-01 to 08-02, 1 829 generations to
step 3 714 in 86 323 s. It had never been compared to anything on a shared scale, for
three reasons that all turned out to be fixable or fatal in a different way than
expected: it was fitted in its own league at 256 eval sims, its intermediate checkpoints
were deleted, and it predates two search changes.

## Finding the one comparison that is not confounded

Counters in the training JSONLs settle what each surviving checkpoint was trained with —
`self_play/collapsed_roots` and `self_play/terminals_truncated` exist only after
`78fb28a` (§6.1a's root terminal sweep) and are non-zero only when §6.6a's node collapse
is on:

| run | sims | root sweep | collapsed roots |
|---|---:|:---:|---:|
| `t12h-n128` | 128 | ✗ | 0 |
| `t24h-n256` | 256 | ✗ | 0 |
| `t9h-n128-sweep` | 128 | ✓ | 0 |
| `t7h-n128-collapse` | 128 | ✓ | 45 646 |
| `t7h-fp8` | 128 | ✓ | 53 393 |
| `t7h-muon` | 128 | ✓ | 50 851 |
| `t24h-fp8` | 128 | ✓ | 283 755 |

So `t24h-n256` cannot be read against `t24h-fp8`, the obvious partner: that would move
sims, the root sweep, node collapse and the fp8 encoder at once. **The one clean pair is
`t12h-n128`**, which shares its search generation exactly and differs in `sims` alone —
and lands within 3.7 % of the same step, because records per generation do not depend on
`sims`, so twice the search buys the same number of positions in twice the time.

| | sims | steps | training s | sweep / collapse |
|---|---:|---:|---:|:---:|
| `t12h-n128` | 128 | 3 583 | 43 088 | ✗ / ✗ |
| `t24h-n256` | 256 | 3 714 | 86 323 | ✗ / ✗ |

⚠️ **Not pristine**: the cosine horizons differ (2 100 steps against 4 000), so
`t12h-n128` spent its last 1 483 steps pinned at `lr_min = 5e-5` while `t24h-n256`
finished on the tail of its schedule. Both end fully annealed; the trajectories are not
identical. This is a consequence of how the two runs were planned, not an independent
choice, and it is the one variable besides `sims` that moved.

## The measurement

One joint fit, 3 players, 3 pairings × 360 games = 1 080 games at **64 eval sims**, 180
distinct openings, 8 random legal plies. One edge resolves ±16 Elo at d = 0.8.
`logs/league-e11-n128-vs-n256.{json,log}`. ⚠️ The eval search runs
`terminal_collapse = False` (the `SearchConfig` default, which the league does not
override), so neither net is evaluated with a feature the other lacks.

| checkpoint | step | train s | Elo | ±95 % | draws |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| anchor | 0 | — | +0 | pinned | 11.0 % |
| `t12h-n128@3583` | 3 583 | 43 088 | **+338** | ±42 | 16.8 % |
| `t24h-n256@3714` | 3 714 | 86 323 | **+613** | ±52 | 13.3 % |

Head to head, 360 games: `t12h-n128` scored **0.143** (17-69-274), which on its own is
**−311 Elo**; the fit puts the gap at **+275 ± 67**. At equal positions, doubling the
simulation budget is worth roughly **+300 Elo** at this point on the curve.

The same two runs on E0's landmark — 25 % of games ending in checkmate, 20-generation
trailing window, last up-crossing:

| | positions to 25 % decisive | wall clock | final decisive | final KL | final plies |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `t12h-n128` | 2.68 M | 1.79 h | 0.651 | 0.186 | 185 |
| `t24h-n256` | **0.79 M** | **1.02 h** | 0.851 | 0.227 | 131 |

**3.4× fewer positions and 1.75× less wall clock.** E1.1's preregistered bar was *"it
must reach the landmark under 1.80 h — i.e. under ~1.41 M positions… a 1.9×
sample-efficiency gain merely to break even."* It cleared both, and 3.4× exceeds the
2.7× the previous doubling gave. `t12h-n128`'s 2.68 M reproduces E0's recorded 2.69 M,
which is the consistency check that the landmark was computed the same way.

## What it does not say

⚠️ **Both nets predate the root terminal sweep and node collapse, and that is not a
neutral omission — it is the reason they are comparable, and possibly the reason the gap
is this large.** §6.1a and §6.6a were built to make a *low*-simulation search find and
play proved wins, which is part of what more simulations buys on its own. The measured
+300 Elo is what `sims = 256` was worth **to the code of 2026-08-01**. On current code
it can only be smaller, and by how much is unmeasured.

⚠️ This is a fresh 3-player fit. Its Elo is comparable **within this report** and to
nothing else — not to the 7 h joint league, not to `t24h-fp8`'s +905.

⚠️ Two endpoints, no curve: the intermediate checkpoints of both runs are gone, so
nothing here constrains the *shape*, only the endpoint.

The Elo-per-hour question is answered only through the landmark's 1.75×, since no
`n = 128` checkpoint exists at 86 323 s on this scale. The landmark overstated the muon
result by ~2.5× when read against an Elo curve
([the muon entry](2026-08-07-muon-curve.md)), so 1.75× is an upper bound on the
wall-clock gain, and the honest reading is: **`sims = 256` is a large sample-efficiency
win and probably, but not provably, a wall-clock win.**

## Why it was asked

E1.2, playout cap randomisation, spends the expensive tier on a fraction of turns. It is
only worth building if the expensive tier has something to sell, and this says it does —
on old code. `logs/curve-t24h-n256.csv`'s own separate-scale numbers are superseded by
the table above for any cross-run purpose.
