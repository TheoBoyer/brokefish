# 2026-08-11 (twelfth entry) — Gumbel: the best network so far, on half the clock

`search.md` §11 has listed `select_root`, `select_interior` and `policy_target` as
seams since the document was written. This is all three, built together because
Gumbel MuZero is one method and not three, and measured against the AdamW/PUCT run
it was designed to replace.

**The result, on the project's own north-star metric — Elo per hour of training:**

| | Elo at n=16 | n=64 | n=256 | training |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `t12h-gumbel@4009` | **824.4 ± 74** | **1014.2 ± 75** | **1425.7 ± 102** | **11.40 h** |
| `t24h-fp8@8416` | 612.4 ± 81 | 890.6 ± 81 | 1348.5 ± 105 | 23.72 h |
| difference | **+212 ± 110** | **+124 ± 111** | +77 ± 147 | **2.08× less** |

`t12h-gumbel@4009:n256` at **1425.7** is the strongest network this project has
measured. Both arms are at their own fully annealed learning rate, so the endpoint
comparison carries no cosine confound, and **PUCT does not reach the 12 h Gumbel
level at any of the three budgets inside its full 24 h.**

## The scale is comparable across leagues, checked rather than assumed

Elo here is self-anchored (`evaluation.md` §5.1: zero is uniformly random legal
play), so a number from one fit means nothing against a number from another unless
the anchor and the fit agree. They do:

| | this league | [the muon league](2026-08-07-muon-curve.md) |
|---|---:|---:|
| `t24h-fp8@8416:n256` | 1348.5 | 1354.5 |

**6 Elo apart on two independent `bt-mm/v1` fits** over different pools. That is what
makes "best measured" a claim about the project and not about one league.

## ⚠️ The preregistration was pinned to the wrong budget, and it was my error

`chain-t12h-gumbel.sh` preregistered, before launch: headline = final checkpoint at
**n = 256**, success = **no regression**, and the deciding output = Elo per doubling
of the evaluation budget.

n = 256 is precisely where Danihelka et al. predict the method washes out — visit
counts become a serviceable improvement operator once the budget is large enough. So
the headline was pinned to the one budget where the effect was expected to be
smallest, and it duly came back **+77 ± 147**, indistinguishable from zero. Reported
as the verdict, which it is not: n = 16 is **+212 ± 110, z = 3.8**.

The grid was preregistered in the same file, so quoting n = 16 is reading the plan
rather than moving the goalposts. The lesson is about *choosing* the headline:
**pin it where the mechanism predicts the effect, not where the previous run's
headline happened to sit.** The n = 256 choice was inherited from the muon chain,
where it was right for a different reason.

## The shape is the paper's, and that is the corroboration

The advantage falls monotonically with the budget: **+212 → +124 → +77** at
n = 16/64/256. That is the published claim — the gains live at small budgets — and
our reproducing its *shape* is stronger evidence the implementation is faithful than
any single Elo number.

It shows up in the slope as the opposite sign to what was predicted before the run:

| Elo per decade of eval budget | 16→64 | 64→256 | 16→256 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `t12h-gumbel@4009` | 315 | 683 | **499** |
| `t24h-fp8@8416` | 462 | 761 | **611** |

⚠️ I wrote the spec expecting a *steeper* small-`n` end. Gumbel's curve is **flatter
at every segment**. Both statements are the same fact: the level at small `n` came
up, so search has less left to add. Going 256 → 16 costs Gumbel 601 Elo and PUCT
736 — 135 Elo less degradation, real but modest.

## What was built

Four pieces, off by default behind `SearchConfig.gumbel`:

1. **Gumbel top-`m` root sampling.** `argtop_m(g + logits)` is exactly a sample of
   `m` actions without replacement from `softmax(logits)`, so the noise *is* the
   sampling and §6.1's Dirichlet does not run.
2. **Sequential halving**, as a host-built `[m+1, budget]` table of required visit
   counts. The elimination is implicit in the visit-count match: a dropped edge
   never again holds the required count.
3. **`softmax(logits + σ(completedQ))` in place of `N / n`**, dense over the whole
   edge set with `v_mix` completing the unvisited.
4. **`π' − N/(1 + ΣN)` below the root**, which removes `FPU_DRAW` from the interior
   entirely — an unvisited edge is scored at `v_mix`, not a constant.

Both implementations: `brokefish/search/gumbel.py` and `torch_impl.py` for the
reference, `gumbel_argmax` in `csrc/search.cuh` for the kernel.

**The record schema did not change.** §10 already stored the root's whole edge set
with `π = 0` on unvisited edges, for the training *denominator* (revised
2026-07-31, for an unrelated reason). Those entries simply stop being zero. No
buffer migration, and `loss.py`'s mass assertion still holds.

## Three things that would have been silent

⚠️ **§6.1a must not seed `edge_N` under Gumbel.** Sequential halving indexes its
schedule by `sum(N)` and eliminates by visit count, so an edge starting a move at
`N = 1` is out of phase with the table for the whole move and is never eligible
again — the budget is misspent and every counter says it worked. The seed is skipped
and only `edge_win` is written; the collapse reads that, and a collapsed root takes
`root_value = 1.0` outright, which is what the seeded `edge_Q` used to carry.

⚠️ **`eval_config` must set `gumbel_scale = 0`.** Gumbel replaces `tau_plies` as well
as the Dirichlet, so at scale 1 every move is a sample forever where v0 goes argmax
past ply 30. `match.py`'s header already states that evaluation takes all of its
diversity from the random openings, so zero costs nothing — and left at 1, a league
would have rated the noise.

⚠️ **`mean_root_edges_visited` was counting the target, not the visits.** It read
`(pi > 0).sum()`, which under PUCT is the visited set because `pi = N/n`, and under
Gumbel is the whole edge list. It reported 28-30 against `t24h-fp8`'s 22-25 at the
same generation, which reads as "Gumbel explores more" and means "mean mobility is
29". Now reads `edge_N`; identical for PUCT, so no measured run moves.

## Cost: none, and the prediction was wrong twice

Interleaved A/B, order-balanced, per `CLAUDE.md`'s rule that a naive before/after
already produced a fake −4.5 % gain:

| | PUCT | Gumbel | |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| kernel, B=512 n=128, fp8 encoder | 880.4 ms/move | 874.5 | **−0.7 % ± 1** |
| reference, B=128 n=64 | 2564 ms/move | 2416 | −5.8 % ± 3 |

Predicted **+4 %** on the kernel from six warp reductions against PUCT's one, and
+10-25 % on the reference. Both wrong in the same direction and for the same reason:
the descent is 4.4 % of a node and the extra arithmetic is register-local warp
reductions hiding under memory latency the descent already pays.

## The differential harness, and where it is weaker than PUCT's

⚠️ **Bit-exactness is not guaranteed here the way it is for §6.6.** PUCT's only
reduction is a sum of non-negative integers, exact in any order. Gumbel needs float
sums — `sum_probs`, the softmax denominator — whose warp-shuffle tree differs from
torch's, so the two can differ in the last ulp.

What makes that survivable is the inverse of what made PUCT hard: at a freshly
created node **every PUCT score is exactly 0**, so ties are the common case and the
tie-break alone decides the first descent below every new node. Gumbel's scores carry
distinct logits and continuous noise, so exact ties are measure zero.

Measured, not argued: five tests, 1088 simulations over four position sets, three
budgets and both rules, and the trees come out **identical** — every §4.2 array, the
training record, the target and `root_value`. Treat that as empirical, not as the
guarantee `test_flat_priors_agree_bit_for_bit` gives PUCT.

⚠️ **An agreement-only test can leave a branch dead and still pass.** The endgame
collapse test proves **0** winning edges under Gumbel where the PUCT run proves 2 —
the two rules descend differently, so `gumbel_argmax`'s collapse branch was never
executed by it. A won-root test was added that puts the ballot on the path and counts
it rather than assuming.

## What the run looked like from inside

At matched step count, against `t24h-fp8`:

| | Gumbel | PUCT |
|---|---:|---:|
| `kl` at step 601 | 1.19 | 0.13 |
| `kl` at the end | 0.72 | — |
| `search_disagrees_frac` | 0.77 | 0.16 |
| `mean_root_max_pi` | 0.60 | 0.17 |
| `mean_root_entropy` | 1.25 | 2.83 |
| `mean_depth` | 5.10 | 2.31 |
| `depth_p99` | 18 | 5 |

⚠️ **`loss` is not comparable across the flag.** The two are cross-entropies against
different targets, whose entropies are 1.25 and 2.83, so the achievable floors differ
by 1.6 nats. Subtracting them gives excess-over-floor of 1.21 and 0.21, which
reproduces the two KLs exactly — that identity is the check that the comparison is
being read correctly.

**The depth difference is the interior rule doing what it is designed to do.** Visit
matching gives the top child roughly `π'(a)` of a node's visits, and with `π'` peaked
at 0.60 that compounds: `128 × 0.6^d = 1` gives `d ≈ 9.5`, against a measured mean of
5-9 and p99 of 12-30. PUCT's `1/(1+N)` decays far faster than proportional, so it
spreads before it descends.

Which makes the other column the striking one. **PUCT at n = 128 searches to a mean
depth of 2** — the root, its children, and a leaf. That is a one-ply value scan, not
a lookahead, and it is consistent with `search_disagrees_frac = 0.16` and `kl = 0.13`:
the search barely changes what the policy already said. "Gumbel is an order of
magnitude deeper" is better read as *our PUCT baseline was not searching*.

## Puzzles, which decided nothing

Layer 1 is diagnosis and may never select (`evaluation.md`; `CLAUDE.md` names
checkpoint selection on puzzle score as the prohibition most likely to be violated).
At matched steps 201/401/601 Gumbel led on every metric and widened — pass@1 0.189
against 0.067 at step 601, 2.8×.

⚠️ It was not treated as evidence at the time, and should not be retrospectively: the
Gumbel target is sharper by construction, and pass@1 rewards a confident argmax.
`pass@5` rose only 1.4× against pass@1's 2.8×, which is the signature of sharpening
rather than knowing. It happened to point the same way as the Elo. That is luck, not
method.

## Two controls, one of which was useless

`t12h-n128@3583` was included to bracket the cosine confound from the other side —
12 h, annealed, same `n`. It is **599 Elo below Gumbel at n = 64**, but also **250
Elo below `t24h-fp8` at the same wall clock while being annealed**, because it
predates fp8 and the terminal collapse and reached 3583 steps against 4185. The
recipe gap swamps the schedule effect and none of that 599 may be attributed to
Gumbel. The bracketing did not happen.

The matched-*step* comparison, `t12h-gumbel@4009` against `t24h-fp8@4009`, is
**+350 ± 108** at n = 64 — and is the one place the launch caveat bites: we are
annealed there and the control is mid-cosine at lr 5.3e-4. **Inflated by an
unmeasured amount, and it does not enter the ledger.**

## What is not established

- **One run per arm, no seed replication.** The muon run led by 2.28× over four
  hours and lost by 152 Elo at 24 h. ⚠️ That specific failure mode does not apply
  here — muon's lead was early and transient, this is an *endpoint* result at half
  the wall clock with a consistent sign at three budgets — but n = 1 is n = 1.
- **The training budget is untested.** Everything above varies the *evaluation*
  budget. Gumbel's promise was that a small `n` becomes viable during self-play,
  which is a throughput claim and needs its own run.
- **Whether 2.08× holds at 24 h.** Measured at one point on the clock.

## Run configuration

`t12h-gumbel`: 12 h, 2059 generations, **4185 steps** against the 4150 a probe sized
(0.8 % out), 174 216 games. Gumbel `m = 16`, `c_visit = 50`, `c_scale = 0.1` (mctx's,
not the paper's — `gumbel.py` is where the discrepancy is written down), uniform
n = 128, AdamW 1e-3 cosine to 5e-5, warmup 30, 1024 games in flight, batch 4096,
window 20 000 games, fp8, terminal collapse. Identical to `t24h-fp8` except for the
search rule and the run length.

⚠️ **AdamW and not muon, deliberately.** Muon measured −152 Elo at 24 h and the
weight-decay question is unresolved; one variable at a time.

The league: 56 players, 317 pairings × 36 games = **11 412 games in 2.36 h**, budget
grid {0, 1, 4, 16, 64, 256}, `random` at zero, `bt-mm/v1`.
`logs/league-joint-12h-gumbel.json`, `logs/curve-joint-12h-gumbel.{csv,png}`.

⚠️ A power cut during the run cost **two generations**: 417 and 418 took 21.4 s and
20.3 s of self-play against the run's flat 18.5-18.6 s, the machine being a laptop
and the battery carrying it. Every other generation of 2059 is within 0.2 s of
nominal.
