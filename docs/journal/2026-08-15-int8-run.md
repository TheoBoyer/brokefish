# 2026-08-15 (eighteenth entry) — int8 buys 1.1× the training and no Elo

`t12h-int8` is the best-known recipe with **one flag changed**, `--fp8` → `--int8`. It
was the first run of the kernel built on 2026-08-14, and it was preregistered against
`t12h-gumbel` — same 12 hours, same cosine, same Gumbel `m = 16`, same `n = 128`, same
window, same batch, same collapse. Only the FFN's precision differed.

The kernel delivered exactly what it was measured to deliver. The Elo did not move.

## What the flag bought

| | `t12h-int8` | `t12h-gumbel` | ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| generations | 2 256 | 2 059 | 1.096× |
| steps | **4 586** | 4 185 | **1.096×** |
| games | **194 227** | 174 216 | **1.115×** |
| play / grad per generation | 16.8 s / 2.2 s | 18.6 s / 2.3 s | |

Both runs ended annealed — `lr` finished at `lr_min = 5e-5`. `--total-steps 4500`
against an actual 4 586 left the last 86 steps flat, which is the safe direction: a
schedule that completes early still anneals, one that does not is not comparable.

## The Elo, and it is a null

One Bradley-Terry fit, 53 players, equal wall clock to 0.2 % (41 146 s against 41 051 s).

| eval budget | `t12h-int8`@4409 | `t12h-gumbel`@4009 | difference | z |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| n = 16 | 1166 ± 81 | 1169 ± 78 | −3 ± 112 | −0.03 |
| n = 64 | 1394 ± 82 | 1343 ± 79 | +51 ± 114 | +0.45 |
| **n = 256** | **1742 ± 94** | **1722 ± 88** | **+20 ± 129** | **+0.16** |

**The preregistered headline is n = 256 and it is +20 ± 129.** The criterion was to
*beat* the control; +20 is not beating anything. Unlike the muon run there is no sign
flip to interpret — all three budgets are uniformly indistinguishable from zero, and
the largest |z| in the table is 0.45.

The compute slopes are unchanged too: +794 / +903 / +1010 Elo per decade of training
seconds at n = 16/64/256, against the control's +810 / +940 / +1001.

## The result was visible hours before the league said it

Two in-run instruments called this correctly and are worth trusting more next time.

**Loss at matched step never separated.** The deltas sign-flipped around zero for the
whole run — −0.094, −0.002, +0.001, −0.002, −0.016, +0.016 over the first 600 steps,
then *worse* for int8 by +0.03 to +0.10 across steps 1800-3400, then back to −0.025 at
the end. That is noise, and it looked like noise at hour two.

**The puzzle probe's early lead evaporated.** ⚠️ +24.2 % relative at step 600, then
+6.0 %, then **+0.1 % at step 2200**, then drifting back to +3.3 % at the end. A real
per-step effect does not vanish and return; that trajectory is what a small probe does
when there is nothing to measure. The probe's record here is now **1-for-4**.

## What this says about the two mechanisms

int8 was expected to help two ways and neither converted.

**Throughput: delivered, and worth nothing measurable.** 1.096× steps and 1.115× games
produced +20 ± 129. ⚠️ This **retires the estimate I gave on 2026-08-14** that 13 % more
games was worth +22 to +42 Elo by applying the +408/+703/+793 per-decade slopes. Those
slopes are per decade of *training seconds*, an axis that does not move when the clock
is held fixed, and applying them to a data increase at constant wall clock was an abuse
of the number. The right prior was [PCR](2026-08-08-pcr-and-the-rebased-scale.md), which
measured **+36 ± 70** for a larger data increase at equal wall clock and could not
distinguish it from zero. It still cannot, and now neither can this.

**Accuracy: no signal at all.** Prior-space error fell 2.56× (max |Δp| 4.37e-2 →
1.71e-2) and move-flip risk from 4.43 % to 1.83 %, and none of it is visible in the
loss, the probe, or the rating. Either a 2.6 % flip rate was never costing much — the
flips are near-ties by construction, so each one is cheap — or the effect is real and
smaller than 130 Elo, which is all this league can resolve.

## What int8 keeps, and why it stays

Nothing here argues against the kernel. It is **bit-identical-verified across nine
networks**, 2.56× more accurate, and **1.36× faster** on the encoder path
(89 265 evals/s, 79 625 in the real MCTS against fp8's 70 367). It costs nothing to
run and it removes a whole class of failure — `kQMax` has no analogue in int8, so the
constant that once produced NaN on 116 boards of 128 cannot exist on this path.

**It stays the default because it is free, not because it buys Elo.** Those are
different claims and only the first is supported.

## Consequence

⚠️ **The best model is ambiguous and I am not calling a new one.** `t12h-int8@4409` is
nominally +20 at n = 256 and inside the noise of `t12h-gumbel@4009`. Two checkpoints
that cannot be told apart are not a new best; they are a tie.

Nothing about the Gate 2 position changes. The head-to-head against AlphaGateau's
released checkpoint was **0.4000 [0.335, 0.469], −70 Elo**, measured at **n = 128 on
both sides** — which is AG's own training and play budget, so that number stands under
the only conditions that matter and there is no protocol left to adjust. A +20 to +51
point estimate against a 70-Elo gap does not change the outlook.

## What is not established

- **Whether the accuracy half is worth anything at all.** A null at ±130 does not mean
  zero; it means smaller than we can see with 36 games per pairing.
- **Whether a longer run would separate them.** Both arms are 12 h, and the throughput
  advantage compounds — 24 h would be 1.1× of a larger number, not a larger ratio.
